# Commander-Facing Influence Report

## Purpose

Convert research and analysis into an operational or executive decision-support product.

## Required scoping check

Resolve or explicitly assume the area of analysis, area of interest, target actor or comparative posture, local and partner actors, mission or decision, planning horizon, required capabilities, audience, sensitivity, classification, and output format. Ask only for missing information that would materially change the report. Never inherit geography, actor, adversary, partner, or mission details from prior outputs.

## Required report structure

1. Title page and analytic status.
2. Executive summary.
3. Key judgments.
4. Commander or executive bottom line.
5. Scope, methodology, assumptions, and confidence.
6. Area strategic context.
7. Freedom-of-maneuver or decision-consequence matrix.
8. DIMEFIL campaign or influence assessment.
9. PMESII-PT × ASCOPE matrix.
10. Actor and relationship-network assessment.
11. Critical nodes, sites, systems, and investments.
12. JIPOE overlay when operationally appropriate.
13. Most likely and most dangerous courses of action.
14. Decision matrix.
15. Indicators and warning.
16. PIRs, EEIs, collection requirements, and gaps.
17. Alternative hypotheses and key assumptions.
18. Lawful engagement, resilience, and mitigation opportunities.
19. Source evaluation and bibliography.

## Required visuals

Use only visuals supported by data:

- Area orientation map.
- Critical-node map.
- Actor–relationship–effect network.
- DIMEFIL or risk heatmap.
- Timeline of agreements, investments, exercises, political transitions, and decision windows.
- Influence-to-operational-effect pathway.
- Indicators and warning dashboard.

Avoid decorative imagery that adds no analytic value.

## Key-judgment format

Each key judgment must contain the judgment, operational or decision consequence, confidence, main evidence, and what would change the judgment.

## Decision matrix

Use columns for decision, required condition, influence or access risk, watch indicator, trigger implication, decision point, and recommended lawful mitigation.

## Writing rules

- Put the decision or operational implication first.
- Use short paragraphs and direct language.
- Avoid doctrinal repetition.
- Define specialized terms once.
- Distinguish fact, inference, assumption, and information gap.
- Use probabilistic language and state confidence at the judgment level.
- Include dates for time-sensitive claims.
- Avoid unsupported allegations about named individuals.

## Activation criteria and non-use cases

Use this skill when a completed or substantially complete influence-and-access baseline must be converted into a commander- or executive-facing product. Do not use it to generate judgments the underlying analysis does not contain, to determine classification or release markings, or to bypass the producing organization's product-line and supervisory review.

## Quality-control and dissemination check

Verify current officeholders and project status, citations, bibliography, classification and sensitivity markings, absence of private personal data, absence of manipulation or covert-action recommendations, protection of operational details, and conclusions proportionate to evidence.

## Stop conditions

Stop and ask the analyst when the underlying baseline is missing or stale for a required section, when a requested visual or judgment is not supported by the evidence base, or when markings, releasability, or audience authorization are unclear.

## Human approval gate

The responsible analyst and the organization's review chain approve the report, its judgments, confidence, visuals, and markings before briefing or dissemination. The dissemination check above is completed as part of this gate; the model must not disseminate the product itself.
