# Joint Intelligence Skills

Human-led skills grounded in publicly releasable joint intelligence doctrine. This namespace supports analysts and planners conducting joint intelligence preparation, intelligence support to joint planning, all-source assessment, indications and warning, collection integration, and decision support.

## Governing approach

Joint doctrine provides a common method and language, but the commander's judgment, current mission guidance, command procedures, classification guidance, and delegated authorities govern execution. These skills do not replace the J-2, intelligence staff, collection managers, targeting authorities, foreign disclosure officers, security managers, or product approval chain.

The assistant must interview the analyst before beginning and confirm:

- the supported command, mission, operation, campaign, or planning effort;
- the commander's decision or staff process being supported;
- priority intelligence requirements and other relevant requirements;
- operational environment, area of operations, area of interest, actors, domains, and time horizon;
- planning assumptions, friendly posture, constraints, and available reporting;
- intended product, audience, classification, releasability, deadline, and review authority.

## Initial skills

- `jipoe-scope-and-operational-environment/` — define the JIPOE problem, scope, baseline, and environmental effects.
- `jipoe-actor-and-course-of-action/` — help the analyst evaluate relevant actors, capabilities, doctrine, centers or systems, potential courses of action, and indicators.

These skills are intentionally modular. They call shared tradecraft for evidence traceability, structured analytic techniques, assumptions, confidence, and assessment language.

## Typical working sequence

1. Interview and confirm the intelligence problem.
2. Establish the information cutoff and source-access limitations.
3. Define the operational environment and significant characteristics.
4. Describe effects on friendly, adversary, neutral, partner, and civil actors.
5. Evaluate relevant actors, capabilities, limitations, doctrine, patterns, and dependencies.
6. Develop and compare plausible courses of action without treating the most likely as the only possible outcome.
7. Derive indicators, information gaps, and collection implications.
8. Integrate findings into planning or decision-support products.
9. Apply ICD 203 and ICD 206 review where applicable.
10. Obtain analyst and organizational approval.

## Public doctrinal baseline

- Joint Publication 2-0, *Joint Intelligence*.
- Joint Publication 2-01, *Joint and National Intelligence Support to Military Operations*, when publicly available and applicable.
- Joint Publication 5-0, *Joint Planning*, for planning integration.
- Current Joint Electronic Library terminology and command-specific guidance.

Always verify the current publication and local implementation before use. Draft doctrine is not authoritative.
