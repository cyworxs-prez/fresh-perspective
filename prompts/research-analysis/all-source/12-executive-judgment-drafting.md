# Executive Judgment Drafting

## Purpose
Convert completed analysis into concise, decision-relevant judgments for any audience or subject.

## Interaction rule
Ask the user to confirm the audience, decision, topic, evidence-backed judgment, confidence, classification or handling limits, desired length, tone, and prohibited terminology. Do not create substantive judgments from an empty evidence base.

## Required parameters
- Intended audience and decision
- Approved analytic judgment and supporting evidence
- Scope and time period
- Confidence level
- Product format, tone, and length
- Handling and citation requirements

## Prompt
Draft three versions of the approved key judgment: approximately 35 words, 75 words, and 150 words, unless the user specifies different lengths.

Each version should identify, where applicable:
- Actor or subject
- Activity or mechanism
- Target, system, or condition affected
- Observed or assessed effect
- Decision consequence
- Time horizon
- Confidence and essential caveat

Then provide:
1. One bottom-line sentence
2. Three priority findings
3. The decision implication for each finding
4. One alternative explanation or key uncertainty
5. The most important collection gap

Use precise language. Distinguish observed facts from judgments and forecasts. Avoid vague labels, unsupported intent, unnecessary background, false precision, deterministic predictions, and policy advocacy disguised as analysis. Preserve the original confidence and caveats rather than strengthening the claim for rhetorical effect.
