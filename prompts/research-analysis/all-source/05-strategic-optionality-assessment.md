# Strategic Optionality Assessment

## Purpose
Assess whether an activity, investment, relationship, capability, or project creates presence, access, influence, dependency, leverage, control, or future options without presuming intent.

## Interaction rule
Ask for missing critical parameters before analysis: target activity, actor set, supported decision, geography, time period, evidence corpus, comparison baseline, and the consequence or vulnerability of concern. Clarify whether the user wants a neutral research assessment, threat assessment, opportunity assessment, or resilience review.

## Required parameters
- Activity, project, investment, relationship, or capability
- Relevant actors and stakeholders
- Decision or research objective
- Scope and time period
- Approved evidence base
- Alternatives or baseline for comparison

## Prompt
Assess the target using the applicable tests below:
- Ownership, governance, and control rights
- Financing, guarantees, collateral, and dependency
- Contracts, concessions, exclusivity, and step-in rights
- Technical, data, physical, institutional, or human access
- Maintenance, supply, standards, and vendor lock-in
- Political, employment, revenue, reputational, or social dependencies
- Dual-use, contingency, or alternative utility
- Replaceability, redundancy, resilience, and recovery time
- Activation triggers and leading indicators
- Observed use during disputes, crises, negotiations, or decisions

Separate:
1. Verified facts
2. Source-reported claims
3. Reasonable inferences
4. Assumptions
5. Unknowns

Develop at least four plausible explanations, including a routine or benign explanation, a commercial or self-interested explanation, a local or stakeholder-driven explanation, and a strategic or leverage-oriented explanation. Add others suited to the topic. For each, identify supporting, contradicting, and diagnostic evidence.

Conclude whether the evidence supports presence, access, positioning, influence, dependency, leverage, control, activation, or none of these. Do not infer malignity or intent from identity, origin, or association alone.
