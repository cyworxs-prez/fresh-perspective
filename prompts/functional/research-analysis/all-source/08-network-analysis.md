# Network Analysis

## Purpose
Map entities and relationships for any adversary, organization, market, ecosystem, issue, or research domain.

## Interaction rule
Ask the user to clarify the focal question, node and edge definitions, scope, time period, approved data, desired level of inference, and whether quantitative network measures are required. Confirm how unidentified or disputed entities should be handled.

## Required parameters
- Focal question and decision relevance
- Entity and relationship dataset or source corpus
- Geographic, organizational, and temporal scope
- Node and edge taxonomy
- Evidence and confidence rules

## Prompt
Create an analytic network from the approved data.

Classify nodes using categories appropriate to the subject, such as government, security, commercial, financial, technical, political, media, academic, civic, criminal, community, individual, infrastructure, or transnational entities.

Classify edges using evidenced relationship types, such as ownership, finance, contract, governance, employment, family, political, information, technical, security, legal, logistical, communication, collaboration, or conflict.

For each node and edge record:
- Unique identifier and aliases
- Type and role
- Direction and strength of relationship
- Start/end dates
- Evidence and source independence
- Confidence and ambiguity

Identify:
1. Central nodes and hubs
2. Brokers, bridges, and gatekeepers
3. Clusters and communities
4. Chokepoints and single points of failure
5. Parallel, redundant, or hidden pathways
6. Temporal changes
7. Potential pathways for information, resources, influence, access, control, risk, or benefit
8. Single-source or weakly supported relationships

Distinguish observed ties from inferred ties. Do not treat association, co-location, shared membership, or indirect contact as proof of coordination or control.
